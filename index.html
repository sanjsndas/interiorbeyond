    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the difference between interior design and interior architecture? +
    Interior design focuses on the aesthetic and functional arrangement of spaces, including furniture, finishes, and color. Interior architecture deals with the structural and spatial modifications of a building's interior — such as moving walls, altering ceiling heights, or designing built-in elements. Both disciplines often overlap in practice.
    How do I choose the right lighting for a small room? +
    Start with layered lighting: a central ambient source (ceiling or pendant), task lighting for specific activities (reading, cooking), and accent lighting to highlight features. Use warm bulbs (2700K–3000K) and consider dimmers to adjust mood. Mirrors and light-colored surfaces can also amplify natural light.
    What are the most sustainable materials for home renovation? +
    Bamboo, reclaimed wood, cork, recycled metal, and low-carbon concrete are excellent choices. Look for materials with high recycled content, low embodied energy, and local sourcing. Also consider durability — the longer a material lasts, the lower its environmental impact over time.
    How can I make a rental apartment feel like home without permanent changes? +
    Focus on removable updates: peel-and-stick wallpaper, renter-friendly flooring (like vinyl tiles), plug-in wall sconces, and large area rugs. Swap hardware on cabinets, add freestanding shelving, and use tension rods for curtains. Layered textiles and art can also transform a space without any drilling.
    What is the ideal ceiling height for a comfortable living room? +
    While 2.7 meters (9 feet) is often considered comfortable, anything above 2.4 meters (8 feet) can work well with proper proportion. Higher ceilings feel more expansive but require careful lighting and furniture placement to avoid a cavernous feel. Lower ceilings can be made to feel intimate with strategic color and lighting.
